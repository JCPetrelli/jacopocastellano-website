Everything you need to know about AI

Bookmark this page. The most important things happening in AI, synthesized from dozens of sources into a two-minute read.

Updated every 48 hours Last updated August 30, 2026 · 42 sources · 154 articles · 48h window View past digests → Subscribe by email Subscribe via RSS

  1. Sony Music and Warner Chappell sue Anthropic over music copyright.

    The publishers filed in the Northern District of California over "tens of thousands" of copyrighted works, seeking up to $150,000 per work plus $25,000 for each instance of stripped copyright data. The suit is unusually broad and centers on accusations of outright piracy rather than fair-use training questions.

    Sources The Verge ·TechCrunch

  2. A federal judge rules the Pentagon's blacklisting of Anthropic illegal.

    The court found the "supply-chain risk" label unlawful, with the national-security rationale assembled only after the decision had already been made, and over Claude capabilities the model did not have. Anthropic had refused to support lethal autonomous weapons and mass surveillance; its second Pentagon lawsuit continues in Washington.

    Sources Ars Technica ·TechCrunch ·The Register

  3. The industry warns AI-powered cyberattacks are months away.

    Over 100 tech companies signed a joint warning of an imminent wave of AI-driven attacks, though critics note the firms selling defenses helped build the problem. The threat is already visible: exploits now appear within minutes of a patch being discussed publicly, and a researcher showed Claude Code can be hijacked simply by asking it to summarize a malicious website.

    Sources Wired ·The Register ·Simon Willison

  4. Tencent releases Hy4 Preview, a 770B-parameter open-weight model.

    The text-only model has 49B active parameters, a 1M token context window, and weighs 1.56TB on Hugging Face: a big jump from July's Hy3 (295B, 256K context). It lands as open-weight AI companies have become Silicon Valley's hottest acquisition targets, with serious capital flowing into giving models away.

    Sources Simon Willison ·Hacker News ·TechCrunch

  5. Capital keeps flooding the physical buildout of AI.

    a16z created a $1.1B "Machine Age" fund aimed at AI hardware and infrastructure, while neocloud Lambda took on $1B in private debt to buy Nvidia chips it leases to Microsoft. Nvidia itself is shifting its advantage beyond the GPU, toward smarter data-center networking and traffic control.

    Sources TechCrunch ·TechCrunch ·TechCrunch

  6. Robots move from demos to payrolls, from car factories to data centers.

    Chinese automakers are following Tesla's bet that humanoid robots are the next big profit machine, and Meta is testing robots that swap cables and reset servers in its data centers, worrying some technicians about their jobs. Consolidation continues too, with EXL acquiring physical-AI model developer iMerit.

    Sources TechCrunch ·Wired ·The Robot Report
