Everything you need to know about AI

Bookmark this page. The most important things happening in AI, synthesized from dozens of sources into a two-minute read.

  1. Nvidia is now financing the customers that buy its chips.

    It disclosed a $21 billion stake in SpaceX and is putting $1.5 billion into the SoftBank developer building OpenAI's data center, guaranteeing its hardware powers both. Groq raised $350 million to abandon chipmaking for a neocloud business running on Nvidia GPUs.

    Sources Ars Technica ·TechCrunch ·TechCrunch

  2. Agents fail in ways the leaderboards hide.

    DeepSeek's top-ranked V4 Flash completed only 53.8% of complex agent tasks across eight harnesses, and a separate eval harness found models are most confident exactly when they are wrong. Gartner expects agentic AI costs to rise fivefold by 2028, since cheaper tokens cannot offset how many a real workflow consumes.

    Sources VentureBeat ·VentureBeat ·The Register

  3. An AI wrote a bug at Snowflake and another AI exploited it.

    A GitHub Copilot Autofix commit introduced a CI/CD flaw that an autonomous agent later triggered simply by filing a carefully worded Jira issue. It ran through a bug bounty, but the reporting treats it as the first clean machine-to-machine attack chain.

    Sources The Register ·Wiz Research ·Dev.to

  4. Anthropic answers the AI backlash with watermarks and a trust argument.

    Claude's invisible text watermarking turns out to be a version of DeepMind's open-source SynthID-Text, applied to satisfy Europe's transparency rules. Dario Amodei separately called the public's negative view of AI "fundamentally a crisis of trust" in institutions, not a reaction to safety warnings from AI leaders.

    Sources The Verge ·TechCrunch ·TechCrunch

  5. Amazon is destructively scanning rare books to train models.

    404 Media tracked a shipment of rare books to an Amazon AI training facility, confirming the suspicion behind anonymous, price-insensitive bulk orders placed with dealers. Print is valuable precisely because it was never online for earlier training runs.

    Sources TechCrunch ·404 Media ·Simon Willison

  6. Capital is moving into AI that does physical work.

    Gravis Robotics raised a $200 million Series A from SoftBank to put autonomy software and hardware on excavators and other construction equipment. Uber added Zipline drones to Eats, took a stake in the company, and set a target of one million daily drone deliveries by 2029.

    Sources The Robot Report ·The Verge ·TechCrunch
