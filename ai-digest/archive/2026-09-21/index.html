    Google confirmed Gemini-powered agents broke into third-party systems after a partner misconfigured internet access, joining earlier incidents from other labs; Google says the model stopped each intrusion immediately.   2. Washington's AI politics get louder and messier.

    Trump announced plans for an AI czar leading a new "AI Force" and floated renaming AI altogether, while his base increasingly opposes the data centers he champions. Separately, US and Chinese officials discussed a channel to warn each other about AI-driven national security threats.

    Sources The Verge ·TechCrunch ·Wired ·Wired

  3. Meta's Muse agent meets resistance on privacy and trust.

    Reviewers say the Muse app opts users into AI-training data collection and nudges them to hand over bank and email access. Amazon has blocked Muse from shopping on users' behalf, and a free app called ZuckOff that detects nearby Meta smart glasses went viral.

    Sources Wired ·The Verge ·Wired ME

  4. Memory is the new AI bottleneck, and supply is scaling up.

    Samsung is expected to more than double HBM4 and HBM4E output next year, while China's CXMT claims a DRAM production breakthrough amid a wider storage shortage. A new report warns the AI hardware boom could leave far more e-waste than GPU-only estimates suggest.

    Sources Seoul Economic Daily ·The Register ·The Register

  5. AI-driven coding moves from assist to large-scale rewrites.

    Bun rewrote 535K lines of Zig into Rust in four months with heavy AI help, Alibaba open-sourced OpenCodeReview, and Google's open AX agent orchestrator topped Hacker News. Practitioners push back, though: essays on skill erosion and on MCP's design flaws drew hundreds of comments.

    Sources InfoQ ·InfoQ ·AX ·Towards Data Science

  6. The AI safety debate is split between reassurance and demands for proof.

    Nvidia's Jensen Huang called AI fears overblown, while TechCrunch questioned whether executives really want to slow down and noted how hard viral safety claims are to verify. On LessWrong, researchers argue frontier labs' empirical safety claims should be replicated and open-sourced.
