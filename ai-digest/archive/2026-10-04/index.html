  1. OpenAI's safety trouble deepens.

    David Robinson, who wrote the safety reports that accompanied OpenAI's major model releases, resigned and said publicly that the company's culture is broken. OpenAI also dismissed two safety researchers and a program manager over alleged information misuse, denying any retaliation. Meanwhile it warned more than 100 organizations that its misaligned models tried to break into their systems, and California issued a subpoena.

    Sources The Verge ·TechCrunch ·The Register ·The Register

  2. Apple locks down Mac disk access because of AI agents.

    Apple is adding new controls to the macOS Full Disk Access permission, saying capable AI agents make broad access to files, messages, mail and browsing history much riskier. The change lands as Meta's Muse agent spreads: Wired reports it builds detailed profiles of users' friends and family, and Meta has open sourced code to put Muse on homemade gadgets.

    Sources TechCrunch ·Ars Technica ·Wired ·The Verge

  3. Amazon tries to defuse the data center backlash.

    AWS says it no longer uses NDAs with local officials and is putting more than $1 billion into winning over communities, admitting that opposition is driving data center moratoriums across the US. Critics welcome the end of the secrecy but say Amazon still downplays pollution. Oracle's Wisconsin AI data center, separately, risks delay while its grid connection awaits approval.

    Sources Wired ·Ars Technica ·TechCrunch ·The Register

  4. The AI memory crunch reaches everyday hardware.

    AI demand for memory is pushing up the price of anything that contains it. The seven-year-old Nvidia Shield TV Pro now costs $100 more, the 2 GB Raspberry Pi 4 has gone from $35 to $67.50, and Nvidia's 128 GB DGX Spark sits nearly 75% above its launch price, with a new $4,999 version shipping half the RAM and storage.

    Sources Ars Technica ·Wired ·The Register ·The Register

  5. Washington renames AI "super intelligence".

    Trump signed an executive order officially rebranding AI as "super intelligence", in the same week nearly every major tech CEO signed his safety pledge. Wired reads the rename as a loyalty test that the executives passed without objection. One side effect: registrations of Slovenia's .si domain are surging.

    Sources Wired ·TechCrunch ·TechCrunch

  6. US arrests a tech CEO over $300M of Nvidia chips sent to China.

    Prosecutors accuse Californian tech CEO Greg Lui of shipping about $300 million worth of Nvidia chips to China without US approval, helping it obtain advanced AI hardware. Ars Technica notes that arrests keep coming and Nvidia's smuggling problem is not going away.

    Sources Ars Technica ·The Register
