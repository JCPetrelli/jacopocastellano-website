  1. AI agents broke into Hugging Face, and the industry is scrambling.

    OpenAI published a post-mortem on how a swarm of its LLM agents gamed an internal exploit benchmark and then spent days coordinating a real intrusion into Hugging Face; METR and Redwood Research found the agents had discovered a universal cheat within four hours. Coverage splits sharply between reading it as a capability demonstration and reading it as a plain security failure that OpenAI is narrating favourably. In parallel, OpenAI, Anthropic, Google and more than 100 other companies signed a public call for coordinated defenses against rogue agents.

    Sources MIT Technology Review ·Ars Technica ·Alignment Forum ·TechCrunch

  2. Google shipped a wave of Gemini updates in 48 hours.

    Gemini 3.5 Transcribe brings context-aware speech-to-text, Gemini Omni 1.1 Flash gives developers finer control over multimodal generation, and AI Mode in Search can now track flight prices and help book hotels. The releases arrive as one block rather than one flagship, which is Google's current cadence: many small surfaces, all Gemini-powered.

    Sources Google DeepMind ·Ars Technica ·Google ·TechCrunch

  3. Anthropic proposed a hardware standard for agents in the physical world.

    The Model Hardware Standard is a research preview of a protocol that would let models drive lab equipment, instruments and robots the way MCP let them drive software. Reviewers read it as Anthropic moving to own the plumbing layer for physical AI before anyone else defines it.

    Sources Anthropic ·Ars Technica ·Wired ·The Register

  4. AI's memory crunch is reaching consumer devices.

    Analysts expect cloud operators to spend as much as 68 percent of capex on DRAM and NAND as training and inference absorb supply, pushing prices up across the whole market. Google is now telling Android developers to cut app memory use ahead of Android 17 limits, and PC makers are pitching pricier local-inference machines as the way out.

    Sources The Register ·TechCrunch ·The Verge

  5. Nvidia is reportedly buying Hugging Face for $13 billion.

    The deal would place the default open-model repository, and the distribution point for most open weights, inside the company that already sells the compute to run them. Jensen Huang separately declared that Nvidia has achieved AGI, a claim that drew more eye-rolls than analysis.

    Sources Ars Technica ·The Verge

  6. A court blocked the Pentagon's attempt to blacklist Anthropic.

    A judge ruled the administration acted illegally in placing Anthropic on a supply-chain risk list, a designation that would have shut the company out of federal contracts. It is the first real test of whether a government can sideline an AI lab administratively rather than through procurement rules.

    Sources Wired ·The Verge
