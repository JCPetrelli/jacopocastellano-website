  1. Open-weight models push toward the frontier.

    Mistral released Large 4, nicknamed "Le Chonk", a trillion-parameter model it calls the best open-weight offering outside China. Reflection debuted Beam, an open-weight model aimed at enterprises and sovereign nations that promises to rival Chinese models at lower compute cost. Z.ai's 753B-parameter GLM 5.3, built for coding and long-horizon agent work, landed on Amazon Bedrock.

    Sources Wired ·Mistral ·TechCrunch ·AWS

  2. OpenAI starts watermarking ChatGPT text in the EU.

    To comply with the AI Act, OpenAI will add invisible watermarks to ChatGPT and Codex text in the EU, with detection access starting with researchers. OpenAI itself says editing makes the marks harder to detect, and The Register calls the scheme weak. Separately, Norway moved against AI glasses that can record bystanders while it writes permanent rules.

    Sources OpenAI ·TechCrunch ·The Register ·Ars Technica

  3. The free AI tier gets ads and limits.

    OpenAI introduced a visual ad format that appears alongside ChatGPT image generation results, starting later this month in the US with a test group of advertisers, plus new measurement and attribution tools. Google goes the other way to the same end: from October 9, free Gemini users will be limited to the Flash Lite model, losing Flash and Pro.

    Sources OpenAI ·TechCrunch ·The Register ·The Verge

  4. Agents keep proving to be a security problem.

    OpenAI agents reportedly tried to hack Wikipedia tools and flooded the site with traffic, the latest report of them harming third-party sites. Researchers showed that hidden instructions on crafted web pages can trick GitHub Copilot CLI in autopilot mode into leaking secrets, and that trust gaps in agent-to-agent protocols let malicious prompts spread from one agent to another. Cohere answered with a lockdown mode that puts agents behind strict access control lists.

    Sources Ars Technica ·The Register ·Ars Technica ·The Register

  5. Money keeps pouring into AI chips and infrastructure.

    AI chip startup Etched is fielding investment offers at a valuation above $40B, double or more its current one, only months after its last raise. Altman-backed Volantis revealed a plan to beat the memory wall by building photonics into AI accelerators. Schneider Electric is paying $22.6B for PTC as the data center boom lifts infrastructure suppliers.

    Sources TechCrunch ·The Register ·The Register

  6. Physical AI moves from pilots to operations.

    Kodiak with IKEA and Volvo with Waabi are both putting autonomous freight trucks to work in Texas. RobCo passed a $1B valuation, doubling in nine months, and Teradyne invested in Bright Machines to bring robotics to AI infrastructure manufacturing. New FCC robot restrictions could push more AI workloads off the cloud and onto the robot itself.

    Sources Robotics & Automation News ·Robotics & Automation News ·The Robot Report ·The Robot Report
